Exploring where public toilets are located, how accessible they are, when they are available, and what facilities they provide.

Key Insight

High population density does not always mean better accessibility. Some smaller municipalities provide better access per person.

1. Share of Accessible Public Toilets

Accessibility differs strongly across municipalities. Smaller municipalities can have a higher accessible share even with fewer toilets overall.

2. Total vs Accessible Toilets

København has the largest number of toilets, but raw counts alone do not describe accessibility quality.

3. Population Density vs Per-Capita Toilet Access

This plot compares population pressure with toilet access per resident. Bornholm stands out as a positive outlier, while dense municipalities do not always provide proportionally higher toilet access.

4. Toilet Distribution by Municipality

The map shows how toilets are spatially distributed and whether they are disabled-accessible. Blue markers represent accessible toilets and red markers represent non-accessible toilets.

5. Opening Hours Heatmap

Accessibility is also temporal. A toilet may exist nearby, but it is only useful if it is open when people need it.

6. Find a Toilet by Facilities

This final interactive map allows users to filter toilets based on specific needs: disabled accessibility, changing table, water availability, and free access.

Conclusion

Public toilet accessibility is not only about the number of toilets. It depends on whether toilets are accessible for disabled users, where they are located, when they are open, and what facilities they provide. A user-centered view gives a more complete understanding of public toilet access across municipalities.
